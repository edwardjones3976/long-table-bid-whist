# The Long Table — Rules Council Brief

**For:** members of the Strict Table Rules Council · **From:** the owner of The Long Table · **Version:** brief-v1.0 (2026-11-20) · **Rules under review:** `strict-table-v1.0.0`

Thank you for agreeing to sit on this council. You are here because you have
played Bid Whist for years at real tables, and we want the game inside The Long
Table to be one you recognise. This brief tells you what we are building, what we
are asking you to judge, how the sessions run, and what happens with your decisions.

Nothing in this document is final until you have spoken.

---

## 1. What The Long Table is

The Long Table is a four-seat Bid Whist card table. It exists today as a phone app
and is being built next as a seated virtual-reality table for Meta Quest. You sit
at South; your partner is North; West and East are the other partnership. The
other three seats can be computer players or, later, invited friends.

The game is one part of a larger idea: a private, lived-in room where people meet
to play, teach, and talk the way they do at a card table at home — not a casino,
not a tournament ladder, not a nostalgia exhibit. The rules have to be right for
that to work, which is why you are here.

## 2. Why a council, and why you

There is no single rulebook for Bid Whist. Families and regions play it
differently — minimum bids, what the jokers do, how "set" is scored, how far the
game runs. Published rules disagree with each other, and none of them is the
authority for the table you grew up at.

So instead of picking rules from a book, we wrote one complete, explicit rule set
— the **Strict Table** — into the game's rules engine, and we are asking a small
council of experienced players to tell us, rule by rule, whether it matches a
table they would sit down at. Where it does not, you tell us what the table you
know does instead.

**You are the authority on the rules.** A separate group tests whether the app is
comfortable and easy to use, and another group advises on the look, sound and
feel of the room. Those are not your job unless something there makes the game
feel wrong as *Bid Whist*; if it does, say so.

## 3. How the council is made up (decision D-03)

- **Five to seven players**, each with at least ten years of regular play.
- **Different tables:** we are deliberately seeking people who learned in
  different cities and different families, so that disagreements between you
  surface the real variants rather than one house's habits.
- **Compensated** for every session and for reading time, at a rate agreed in
  writing before the first session.
- **Independent:** no one on the council works on the game, and no one on the
  game team votes. The facilitator runs the clock and takes notes, nothing more.
- **Separate** from the usability testers and the cultural/art advisors, so rule
  decisions are never traded against comfort or aesthetics.

## 4. The Strict Table, rule by rule

This is exactly what the game enforces today. Please read it as you would a
house's rules sheet pinned above the table. For each line, the response form
(§8) asks: *Is this how your table plays? If not, what does yours do?*

### 4.1 The deal

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R1 | A 54-card deck: the standard 52 plus a **Big Joker** and a **Little Joker**. |
| R2 | Four players in two partnerships: **South & North** against **West & East**. |
| R3 | Each player is dealt **12 cards**; **6 cards** are dealt face-down to the **kitty**. |
| R4 | The deal rotates clockwise each hand. The first dealer of a match is East, so South speaks first. |

### 4.2 The bidding

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R5 | Bidding starts with the player to the dealer's left and goes clockwise. **Each player speaks once** — one round. |
| R6 | A bid is a number from **4 to 7**, plus **Uptown** or **Downtown**, plus **Trump** or **No-Trump**. There is no 3-bid. |
| R7 | A higher number outranks a lower one. At the **same number, No-Trump outranks a suit bid**; a suit bid never outranks No-Trump at the same number. |
| R8 | If the first three players pass, the **dealer may not pass** and must bid at least 4. ("Stuck as dealer.") |
| R9 | The bidder's **Uptown/Downtown and Trump/No-Trump are binding** from the moment they are spoken. After winning, a suit bidder names **only the suit**; nothing else can change. |

### 4.3 The kitty

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R10 | The bid winner picks up the kitty **privately** (the other three never see it), now holds 18 cards, and lays **6 cards face-down**. Discards are never shown. |
| R11 | The kitty counts as the **bid winner's partnership's first book**. A hand therefore has **13 books**: the kitty plus 12 tricks. |
| R12 | The bid winner **leads the first trick**. |

### 4.4 Card ranking and the jokers

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R13 | **Uptown:** Ace high, then King, Queen, Jack, 10 … down to 2. |
| R14 | **Downtown:** **Ace is still highest**, then **2** is next strongest, then 3, 4 … up to King, which is lowest. |
| R15 | In a **trump** hand, the **Big Joker is the highest trump** and the **Little Joker the second highest**, above the Ace of trumps. A joker is a trump card for following suit. |
| R16 | In a **No-Trump** hand, the jokers have **no suit and cannot win a book**: any suited card beats them. |
| R17 | In a No-Trump hand a joker **may be led**. It sets no suit; the next player may play anything, and the **first suited card** played is the suit the rest of the table must follow. |

### 4.5 Play

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R18 | You **must follow suit** if you can. If you cannot, you may play anything, including trump. |
| R19 | A book is won by the **highest trump** if any trump was played, otherwise by the **highest card of the suit led**. The winner leads the next book. |
| R20 | There is **no renege penalty** in the game because the game will not let a player renege: cards that would break suit are locked. |

### 4.6 Scoring

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R21 | **Making the bid** scores the number bid (a 5 bid = 5 points) for the bidding partnership. |
| R22 | A **No-Trump** bid made scores **double** (5 No-Trump = 10 points). |
| R23 | **Being set** gives the **same number of points to the opponents**. Nobody's score ever goes **below zero**. |
| R24 | A **Boston** — the bidding side takes **all 13 books** — scores **14 points**, but **only on a 7 bid**. A Boston on a lower bid scores as an ordinary made bid. |
| R25 | The first partnership to **7 points** wins the match. |

### 4.7 The table

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R26 | A hand may be **voided** (thrown in and redealt with no points moving) only if **every human player agrees**, and only while the hand is still in play. The reason is written in the table's record. Nobody — not even the host — can void a hand alone. |
| R27 | The host of a table can pause between hands, name the computer players, and set how chatty they are. The host **never** sees anyone's cards and **never** touches a hand in progress. |

## 5. Questions we most need your judgement on

These are the places where published rules and tables we have heard about
disagree. Please answer each from the table you know, not from what you think
is "official".

1. **Minimum bid (R6).** Is 4 the lowest bid at your table, or do you allow 3?
2. **One round of bidding (R5).** Does each player speak exactly once, or can bidding go around again?
3. **No-Trump outranks a suit bid at the same number (R7).** Is that your rule, or is a suit bid of the same number allowed to overcall?
4. **Stuck dealer (R8).** Must the dealer bid if everyone else passes, and if so, is the minimum 4?
5. **Binding declaration (R9).** At your table, does the bidder say Uptown/Downtown (and Trump/No-Trump) *with* the bid, or only after winning it? Can a suit bidder switch to No-Trump after winning?
6. **Downtown ranking (R14).** Is the Ace **high** in Downtown at your table, or does Downtown put the Ace at the bottom? Is the 2 the next strongest card?
7. **Jokers in No-Trump (R16–R17).** At your table in No-Trump: are the jokers dead cards, are they removed from the deck, or do they still have some value? Is a player allowed to *lead* a joker?
8. **The kitty as a book (R11).** Does the kitty count as the bidder's first book? Is the kitty ever shown face-up ("sporting" it)?
9. **Set scoring (R23).** When the bidding side is set, do the opponents **gain** the points, or does the bidding side **lose** them (going negative)?
10. **No-Trump doubling (R22).** Does No-Trump double the points at your table?
11. **Boston (R24).** What is a Boston at your table — all 13 books, or something else? What is it worth, and does it need a 7 bid?
12. **Game (R25).** Do you play to 7, or another number? Is there a "double game" or "rise and fly" convention?
13. **Reneging (R20).** Since the game cannot let a renege happen, are we losing a part of the game you value (the penalty of three books, the argument), or is enforcement welcome?
14. **Anything missing.** Is there a rule at your table that this sheet does not mention at all?

## 6. How a session runs

Three sessions of about ninety minutes, in person or by video, four to six weeks
apart. You may bring your own deck.

1. **Session 1 — the sheet.** We walk the rules in §4 one at a time. For each,
   every member gives **one independent position** before any discussion, then
   one short rebuttal round, then we record where the council stands.
2. **Session 2 — the hands.** You play six prepared hands against the app's
   computer players (and each other) and tell us where what you see on the table
   differs from what the sheet says, or from what you expected. We also replay
   the bidding and play of three recorded hands so you can check the table's
   decisions book by book.
3. **Session 3 — the ruling.** You settle the open questions from §5 and sign
   off the manifest, or send it back with changes.

**Decision rule.** For each rule the council records one of:

- **ACCEPT** — this is a table we recognise; keep it as written.
- **CHANGE** — the rule is wrong for the table we would sit at; here is what it should say.
- **VARIANT** — more than one real tradition exists; keep this as the Strict Table default and offer the other as a named, declared option (never a hidden toggle).

A rule changes only when a **majority** of the council votes CHANGE. Ties stay
as written and are recorded as open. The facilitator does not vote. Dissent is
recorded by name with the dissenter's wording.

## 7. What happens to your decisions

- Every ACCEPT, CHANGE and VARIANT is written into the project's record with
  the names of those who voted and the reasons given.
- CHANGEs become a new rules version (`strict-table-v1.1.0`) and are
  implemented and tested before the next session, so you see them on the table.
- VARIANTs become clearly labelled table presets that a host picks before a
  game, with the full rules sheet shown and acknowledged before the first deal.
- Where the council and the rules sheet disagree about **how real tables are
  played**, the council wins. Where a disagreement is about **how the software
  should behave** (for example, that it will not let a player renege), the owner
  decides and records why.
- You will be credited in the app by name if you wish, or not at all if you prefer.

| R1 … R27 | Yes / No / Varies | | Certain / Fairly sure / Unsure |

Then the fourteen questions in §5, each with: *Your table's rule · Where you learned it (city, decade, family or club) · How common you believe it is.*

## 9. Practical matters

- **Compensation:** paid per session plus a reading stipend, agreed in writing before Session 1.
- **Recording:** sessions are audio-recorded for note-taking only, with your consent, and recordings are deleted after the written record is agreed. Nothing you say is used in the app's marketing without separate written permission.
- **Confidentiality:** the app is unreleased. We ask you not to share screenshots or builds. You are free to talk about Bid Whist, of course.
- **Accessibility:** tell the facilitator what you need — large print, a reader, a break schedule, a quieter room — and it will be arranged.
- **Questions:** contact the owner directly at any time between sessions.
