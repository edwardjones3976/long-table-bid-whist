# The Long Table — Rules Council Brief

**For:** members of the Strict Table Rules Council

**From:** the owner of The Long Table

**Date:** 20 November 2026 (brief, second draft)

**Rules under review:** the Strict Table rules sheet, first edition

Thank you for agreeing to sit on this council. You are here because you have
played Bid Whist for years at real tables, and we want the game inside The Long
Table to be one you recognise. This brief tells you what we are building, what we
are asking you to judge, how the sessions run, and what happens with your decisions.

No rule in this document is final until you have spoken.

---

## 1. What The Long Table is

The Long Table is a four-seat Bid Whist card table. It exists today as an app for
an ordinary phone or tablet, and it is being built next as a seated
virtual-reality table for a headset. You sit at South; your partner is North; West
and East are the other partnership. Today the other three seats are computer
players; later they can be invited friends.

The game is one part of a larger idea: a private, lived-in room where people meet
to play, teach, and talk the way they do at a card table at home — not a casino,
not a tournament ladder, not a nostalgia exhibit. The rules have to be right for
that to work, which is why you are here.

## 2. Why a council, and why you

You know better than we do that there is no single rulebook for Bid Whist.
Families and regions play it differently — minimum bids, what the jokers do, how
being set is scored, how far the game runs — and published rules disagree with
each other.

So instead of picking rules from a book, we wrote one complete, explicit rule set
— the **Strict Table** — into the game itself, where it cannot be bent during
play, and we are asking a small council of experienced players to tell us, rule
by rule, whether it matches a table they would sit down at. Where it does not,
you tell us what the table you know does instead.

**You are the authority on the rules.** A separate group tests whether the app is
comfortable and easy to use, and another group advises on the look, sound and
feel of the room. Those are not your job unless something there makes the game
feel wrong as *Bid Whist*; if it does, say so.

## 3. Who is on the council and how it works

- **Five to seven players**, each with at least ten years of regular play.
- **Different tables:** we are deliberately seeking people who learned in
  different cities and different families, so that disagreements between you
  surface the real variants rather than one house's habits.
- **Compensated** for every session and for reading time, at a rate and payment
  schedule agreed in writing before the first session. Travel for in-person
  sessions is reimbursed. A session cancelled by us is paid.
- **Independent:** no one on the council works on the game, and no one on the
  game team votes. The facilitator runs the clock, reads out the forms and takes
  notes, nothing more. The owner attends to answer factual questions about what
  the game does when the facilitator asks, does not speak during positions,
  rebuttal or voting, and does not argue for any rule.
- **Separate** from the usability testers and the cultural/art advisors, so rule
  decisions are never traded against comfort or aesthetics.
- **Leaving and replacement.** You may leave at any time by telling the
  facilitator; no reason is needed, you are paid for work done, and your recorded
  votes stand unless you ask for them to be withdrawn. The owner may remove a
  member only for conduct towards other members, never for their positions on
  the rules, and the reason is recorded and shown to the council. A replacement
  is seated only before Session 2, so that everyone who rules in Session 3 has
  seen the hands.
- **Quorum.** Votes are counted among members present; a vote needs at least
  five members present to be final. If fewer are present, or the council has
  fallen below five, the vote is recorded as provisional and confirmed in
  writing by every member before anything changes in the game.

## 4. The Strict Table, rule by rule

This is exactly what the game enforces today. Please read it as you would a
house's rules sheet pinned above the table. For each line, the response form
(Section 8) asks: *Is this how your table plays? If not, what does yours do?*

### 4.1 The deal

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R1 | A 54-card deck: the standard 52 plus a **Big Joker** and a **Little Joker**. |
| R2 | Four players in two partnerships: **South & North** against **West & East**. |
| R3 | Each player is dealt **12 cards**; **6 cards** are dealt face-down to the **kitty**. |
| R4 | The deal rotates clockwise each hand. The first dealer of a match is East, so South speaks first. |

### 4.2 The bidding

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R5 | Bidding starts with the player to the dealer's left and goes clockwise. **Each player speaks once** — one round. |
| R6 | A bid is a number from **4 to 7**, plus **Uptown** or **Downtown**, plus **Trump** or **No-Trump**. There is no 3-bid. |
| R7 | A higher number outranks a lower one. At the **same number only one overcall is allowed: No-Trump over a suit bid.** A suit bid cannot overcall a suit bid, No-Trump cannot overcall No-Trump, and Uptown or Downtown makes no difference to rank. |
| R8 | If the first three players pass, the **dealer may not pass** and must bid at least 4. ("Stuck as dealer.") |
| R9 | The bidder's **Uptown/Downtown and Trump/No-Trump are binding** from the moment they are spoken. After winning, a suit bidder names **only the suit**; nothing else can change. |

### 4.3 The kitty

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R10 | A suit bidder **names trump before touching the kitty**; the kitty stays face-down until the suit is called. A No-Trump bidder picks it up straight away. The bid winner picks up the kitty **privately** (the other three never see it), now holds 18 cards, and lays **any 6 of them face-down — kitty cards included**. Discards are never shown. |
| R11 | The kitty counts as the **first book for the side that won the bid**. A hand therefore has **13 books**: the kitty plus 12 tricks. |
| R12 | The bid winner **leads the first trick**. |

### 4.4 Card ranking and the jokers

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R13 | **Uptown:** Ace high, then King, Queen, Jack, 10 … down to 2. |
| R14 | **Downtown:** **Ace is still highest**, then **2** is next strongest, then 3, 4 … up to King, which is lowest. |
| R15 | In a **trump** hand, the **Big Joker is the highest trump** and the **Little Joker the second highest**, above the Ace of trumps. A joker is a trump card for following suit: a joker led is a trump lead, and trump must follow. |
| R16 | In a **No-Trump** hand, the jokers have **no suit and cannot win a book**: any suited card beats them. |
| R17 | In a No-Trump hand a joker **may be led**. It sets no suit: everyone is free to play anything until the first suited card lands, and that card's suit is the one the rest of the table must follow. The book goes to the highest card of that suit; the joker cannot win it. |

### 4.5 Play

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R18 | You **must follow suit** if you can. If you cannot, you may play anything, including trump. |
| R19 | A book is won by the **highest trump** if any trump was played, otherwise by the **highest card of the suit led**. The winner leads the next book. |
| R20 | There is **no renege penalty**, because the game does not let a renege happen: when you hold the suit led, only cards of that suit can be played. |

### 4.6 Scoring

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R21 | **Making the bid** scores the number bid (a 5 bid = 5 points) for the bidding partnership. |
| R22 | A **No-Trump** bid made scores **double** (5 No-Trump = 10 points). |
| R23 | **Being set** gives the opponents **the points the bid would have earned** — 5 for a set 5 bid, 10 for a set 5 No-Trump. Nothing is taken off the bidding side, so nobody's score ever goes **below zero**. |
| R24 | A **Boston** — the bidding side takes **all 13 books** — scores **14 points**, and only on a 7 bid. Because a 7 bid can only be made by taking every book, **a made 7 bid always scores 14**, whether it is a suit bid or No-Trump (7 No-Trump is 14, not 28; the Boston bonus takes the place of the No-Trump double). A Boston on a lower bid scores as an ordinary made bid. |
| R25 | The first partnership to **7 points** wins the match. |

### 4.7 The table

| # | Rule as the game plays it |
| --- | --- |
| R26 | A hand may be **thrown in** with no points moving only while it is still in play — from the moment it is dealt until the last card of the last book hits the table — and only when **every human player at the table agrees**; the computer players never object. Once the last card is down the hand can only be scored, and any request still waiting is dropped. The request and each agreement are written in the table's record. **In today's one-human table that means the host alone can throw a hand in (Request, then Confirm), and the record notes a standard reason.** Once friends can sit at the table, every one of them must agree first. The deal then passes to the next player, the same as after a scored hand; the dealer does not deal that hand over. |
| R27 | The host of a table can hold the next deal (a pause between hands), name the computer players, and set how much they talk (Quiet, Social or Lively). The host **never sees anyone's cards** and cannot stop or throw in a hand on their own; the one thing a host may do to a hand in progress is ask the table to throw it in under R26. |

## 5. Questions we most need your judgement on

These are the places where published rules and tables we have heard about
disagree. Please answer each from the table you know, not from what you think
is "official". Three-handed (cutthroat), six-hand two-deck and multi-table
tournament formats are outside what this table will play; please keep to the
four-hand partnership game.

1. **Minimum bid (R6).** Is 4 the lowest bid at your table, or do you allow 3?
2. **One round of bidding (R5).** Does each player speak exactly once, or can bidding go around again?
3. **Overcalling at the same number (R7).** At your table, does No-Trump beat a suit bid of the same number? Does Downtown outrank Uptown at the same number, or make no difference?
4. **The dealer's last word (R7–R8).** If the first three players pass, what happens at your table: the dealer must bid (minimum 4? 3?), or the hand is thrown in and redealt? Separately, when others have bid, may the dealer **take the bid at the same number** instead of going higher, or must the dealer outbid like everyone else?
5. **What is said with the bid, and when the suit is named (R9–R10).** At your table, what does a bid contain: (a) number, direction and Trump/No-Trump all together ("five uptown", with the suit named later); (b) number only for a suit bid, with direction said only on a No-Trump bid ("four", "four no low"); or (c) number only, everything else named after winning? After winning a suit bid, does the bidder name trump **before** looking at the kitty, or **after**? Can a suit bidder switch to No-Trump after winning? (Today the game requires (a), names trump before the kitty is seen, and allows no switch.)
6. **Downtown ranking (R14).** Is the Ace **high** in Downtown at your table, or does Downtown put the Ace at the bottom? Is the 2 the next strongest card?
7. **Jokers (R1, R16–R17).** Does your table play with two jokers, one, or none (52 cards and a 4-card kitty)? In No-Trump, are the jokers dead cards that lose to anything, or do they still have some value? If a player who is not the bidder holds a joker in a No-Trump hand, what happens — it is played as a dead card, swapped, or the hand is redealt? May a joker be **led**, and if so what suit must follow?
8. **The kitty (R10–R11).** Does the kitty count as the bidder's first book? Is the kitty ever shown face-up ("sporting" it) — always, only on No-Trump bids, or never? Does your bidder name trump before or after looking at the kitty?
9. **Being set (R23).** When the bidding side is set, do the opponents **gain** the points, or does the bidding side **lose** them and go **in the hole** (below zero)? Can a partnership lose the game by going too far in the hole?
10. **No-Trump points (R22).** Does No-Trump double at your table? If so, does it double **both** when the bid is made and when it is set? (Today the game doubles both: 5 No-Trump set gives the opponents 10.) And when a bid is made, do you score the **number bid**, or **every book over six you actually took**?
11. **Boston (R24).** What is a Boston at your table — all 13 books, or something else? What is it worth? Does it need a 7 bid, or is bidding 7 the same thing as bidding Boston? Is a 7 No-Trump Boston worth the same 14, or is it doubled? Does a Boston win the game by itself?
12. **Game (R25).** How many points is game at your table — 7, 9, 11, 15, 21, or another number? Does a side also **lose** by going to minus that number? (Rise and fly — the losing pair leaves the table and the next pair sits down — is a party convention we will ask about separately; this table seats one four-hand game.)
13. **Looking back at books (R19).** May a player look at a book once it is turned? Only the last book, and only until the next card is led? Never? May a player ask how many books each side has, or what is trump?
14. **Reneging (R20).** The game will not let a renege happen. Does your table lose something it values when that is taken away — the three-book penalty, the skill of catching one, the call itself — or would you welcome it?
15. **After a hand is thrown in (R26).** Does the same dealer deal again, or does the deal pass?
16. **Anything missing.** Is there a rule at your table that this sheet does not mention at all?

## 6. How a session runs

Three sessions of about ninety minutes, in person or by video, four to six weeks
apart, followed by a short written confirmation round. You may bring your own deck.

1. **Session 1 — the sheet.** Your completed response form (Section 8) *is* your
   independent position; please fill it in before the session — this is paid
   reading time. At the session the facilitator reads out, rule by rule, how the
   forms came in. Where every form says *Yes*, the rule is read aloud once and
   recorded ACCEPT unless anyone asks to open it. Where forms disagree, each
   member who marked *No* or *Depends on the table* says in a minute what their
   table does, there is one short rebuttal round, and the council votes. Rules not
   reached in ninety minutes carry to a second sitting of Session 1, not into
   Session 2.
2. **Session 2 — the hands.** We have prepared six hands in advance (the game can
   replay a chosen deal exactly). You can take them two ways, and you may mix them:
   - **On paper, at a real table.** Each hand comes as a printed deal sheet —
     every seat's 12 cards and the 6 kitty cards. Deal them out with a deck and
     play or talk the hand through as a table. We then show you, book by book,
     what the game did with the same cards, and you tell us where the game's
     table differs from yours.
   - **On a phone or tablet.** The game today runs on an ordinary phone or tablet,
     not a headset — nobody on this council will be asked to wear a headset. You
     sit at South against three computer players; the other seats cannot yet be
     taken by people, so you cannot play each other in the app. One member plays
     at a time with the screen shown to the whole council; the facilitator will
     work the device for anyone who prefers to call the cards.
   We also replay the bidding and play of three recorded hands so you can check
   the table's decisions book by book.
3. **Session 3 — the ruling.** You settle the open questions from Section 5 and
   vote the remaining rules. Signing the record means "this is an accurate record
   of how I voted and what I said", not that you agree with every rule; dissent is
   attached to the same page.
4. **Confirmation round.** Changes decided at Session 3 are built and sent to you
   as a short written and video record of three hands within four weeks. You
   confirm by reply (paid as reading time) or ask for a fourth short session,
   which we will hold.

**Decision rule.** For each rule the council records one of:

- **ACCEPT** — this is a table we recognise; keep it as written.
- **CHANGE** — the rule is wrong for the table we would sit at; here is what it should say.
- **VARIANT** — more than one real tradition exists; keep this as the main Strict
  Table rule and offer the other as a named choice the host can pick, shown to
  the whole table before the first deal — never buried in a settings menu. If a
  majority would rather the *other* tradition be the main rule, that is a CHANGE,
  not a VARIANT.

A rule changes only when a **majority of members present** votes CHANGE. Ties
stay as written and are recorded as open. The facilitator does not vote.
Abstentions are recorded and do not count toward the majority. Dissent is
recorded with the dissenter's own wording.

**Wording and the record.** When the council votes CHANGE, the facilitator reads
the proposed new wording back and the members who voted for it confirm it before
it is recorded; the rule is built in those words. Within seven days of each
session every member receives the written record of positions, votes and
wording. You have fourteen days to correct anything attributed to you;
corrections are made, and any disagreement about what was said is noted in the
record itself.

## 7. What happens to your decisions

- Every ACCEPT, CHANGE and VARIANT is written into the council record with the
  votes and the reasons given.
- CHANGEs become the **second edition of the rules sheet**, built and tested
  before you see them again in the confirmation round.
- VARIANTs become clearly named rules sheets that a host chooses before a game;
  whichever sheet is in play is shown to everyone at the table and agreed before
  the first card is dealt.
- Where the council and the rules sheet disagree about **how real tables are
  played**, the council wins. Where a disagreement is about **how the software
  should behave** — for example, whether the game should ever allow a renege to
  happen — the owner decides and records why, and your view on it (Question 14)
  is recorded alongside.
- **Your name.** We keep two records. The *council record* (how each member voted
  and why) is private to the owner, the facilitator and the council, and is kept
  at least as long as the game is maintained. Anything *public* — the game's
  credits, a published rules history, a website — uses your name only if you
  tick that box on the consent form; otherwise it says "a council member". You
  can change either choice at any time by telling the facilitator, and the
  public versions will be updated. Dissent appears in public documents by name
  or as "one member", as that member chooses.

- *How common do you think it is?*

## 9. Practical matters

- **Compensation:** paid per session plus a reading stipend, with the amounts and
  payment dates agreed in writing before Session 1.
- **Recording:** sessions are audio-recorded for note-taking only, with your
  consent. If any member declines, that session is not recorded and the
  facilitator takes written notes instead. Any member may ask for the recorder to
  be stopped at any time. Video sessions are recorded as audio only. Recordings
  are deleted once the written record for that session has been agreed (Section
  6). Nothing you say is used in the app's marketing without separate written
  permission.
- **Confidentiality:** the game is unreleased. We ask you not to share pictures or
  video of the game, or copies of the app itself, until it is released. You are
  free to say that you advise the project, and free to talk about Bid Whist, of
  course. Any device lent for Session 2 is returned at the end of the session and
  records only the hands played on it.
- **Accessibility:** tell the facilitator what you need — large print, a reader,
  a break schedule, a quieter room — and it will be arranged.
- **Questions:** send any question about the rules, the sessions or the game to
  the facilitator. Questions and answers about the rules are shared with the
  whole council so everyone hears the same thing. Questions about pay, access or
  anything personal go to the owner and stay private.
