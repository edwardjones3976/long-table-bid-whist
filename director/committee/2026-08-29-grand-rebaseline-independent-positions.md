## Claude — VOTE=C

Claude recommends a deliberately narrow staged hybrid that is closer to native-only than to a conventional three-product strategy:

- Native Quest is the only flagship and the only place the authoritative embodied game is played.
- The current rules kernel, typed intent vocabulary, idempotent input IDs, and structural South/public projection should be extracted behind a platform-neutral authority boundary rather than reimplemented in C#.
- WebXR is demoted to tutorial, host console, public spectator projection, and no-headset demonstration. It may add zero gameplay logic and may receive only structural projections that contain no concealed hand.
- Horizon Worlds receives no product approval. A single parity spike must prove concealed state, host authority, card interaction, haptics, and performance. Failure permanently demotes it to a non-gameplay front porch or removes it.
- Hands are the emotional hero; controllers have full reliability parity. The existing preview → cancel/confirm semantics become physical lift → preview detent → explicit commit.
- The largest strategic moat is a trustworthy, hostable, rules-correct Bid Whist night rather than a broad casino or social metaverse.

Claude's strongest case against its own vote is that staged hybrid may become a promise the team cannot enforce. If companion and Horizon non-goals cannot be enforced by review, Claude says A is the better choice.

## Grok — VOTE=A

Grok recommends a native-only Quest flagship and treats hybrid as the committee's most dangerous consensus trap:

- The product's highest-weight qualities—embodied interaction, concealed-state integrity, and safety/privacy—require native control.
- A host-led niche product for adults 30–70 may not need Worlds foot traffic. The host who fills a Saturday table is the acquisition channel.
- “Staged” controls time, not surface count. A native app, World, and WebXR companion still create three UX metaphors, three QA programs, two front doors, and additional privacy/right surfaces.
- WebXR should remain an internal rules harness, not a public tutorial, host console, or live spectator surface until every public projection is proven incapable of revealing concealed state.
- Hands are the emotional hero and controllers the reliability hero; both must implement the same deal, fan, preview, commit, and safe tracking-loss behavior.
- The spectacle should be intimate: passthrough table, deal, fan, partnership talk, robot dealer, and a human host who can run the night but cannot cheat.

Grok's strongest case against A is acquisition. If a real host cannot get four adults through native installation and setup or cannot produce a second hosted night, discovery becomes existential and a non-playable World or web landing surface may be justified.
