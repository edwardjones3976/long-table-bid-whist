## 1. Recommended milestone direction

Use a paired two-stage proof of one rules engine:

- **Stage A:** deterministic, rendering-independent Bid Whist logic exercised
  through a browser UI for deal → bid → play → score under one named manifest.
- **Stage B:** the same logic, not a reimplementation, driving a minimal
  seated Quest room with user Meta avatars, controller input, and one complete
  hand with no artificial locomotion.

Neither stage alone validates the product; the milestone completes only when
both succeed against the same rules core.

## 2. Strongest rejected alternative

Reject treating the browser proof as sufficient evidence for Quest comfort,
avatars, hand/controller parity, spatial voice, privacy, or networking. Also
reject a Quest-only start: the deterministic rules core is faster and safer to
iterate in a browser harness before device integration.

## 3. Scope

**In:** one named default variant; four seated players; one complete hand; user
Meta avatars; optional robot AI Dealer; optional limited Human Host;
controller-complete input with hand parity for bid/play/pass; captions; native
mute/block/leave/report/pause; private invite-only sessions; local comfort
settings; music off by default.

**Deferred:** variant library, public matchmaking, spectators, tournaments,
persistent profiles, cross-platform play, bundled music without provenance,
host-audio ingestion, and monetization.

## 4. Authority concerns

- No role may access concealed information beyond the acting seat's legal
  projection; make this structural, not a policy promise.
- AI enforcement should be preventive—reject illegal bids/plays before commit—
  rather than after-the-fact adjudication of human disputes.
- Human Host powers cover conduct and between-hand operations. Player
  substitution requires a hard disconnect/leave condition, not discretion to
  replace a present compliant player.
- Refuse even well-intentioned “help” features that let a host view a player's
  hand.

## 5. Experience and rights decisions

- Controller-complete baseline; hands are an equal convenience layer.
- Confirm before locking a bid or playing a card.
- Caption every dealer/system utterance; keep text/card scale local.
- Safety controls must exist and be manually verified before external human
  play.
- Use no invented demographic avatars. Cultural warmth requires human review
  by cultural advisors and experienced players; research cannot self-certify it.
- Music defaults off. Any bundled ambience needs a signed provenance record.
  A later host-music path must be isolated from recording and redistribution.

## 6. Proof order and tools

1. Engine-agnostic deterministic rules module and transition tests.
2. Browser interaction proof.
3. Quest-native seated slice reusing the same module.
4. Private invite/session plumbing.
5. Social-safety controls verified before external playtest.

Prefer a standalone rules core callable by both the web harness and the
Quest/Meta Presence and Avatar integration so gameplay cannot silently diverge.

## 7. Acceptance gates

1. Complete legal/illegal transition suite for the named manifest.
2. One full hand in browser and Quest using the identical core.
3. Mute, block, leave, report, pause, and remove manually verified before
   external participation.
4. Experienced-player, cultural-advisor, accessibility, comfort, and
   legibility human gates.
5. Zero uncleared bundled audio; provenance for any ambience; no recording or
   redistribution in a host-external audio path.

## 8. Top risks

1. False equivalence between browser completion and headset/social evidence.
2. Authority creep that weakens hidden-information trust.
3. A music-rights policy stated in prose but not enforced architecturally.

## 9. Advisory vote

`RESHAPE`: preserve the concept, but make the paired proof, authority matrix,
and enforceable rights boundary milestone gates rather than downstream wishes.
