## Position

1. **Product direction:** Preserve the serious private long-table experience. Human presence should come from each participant's Meta avatar rather than an authored cast. Let Foundational Black American influence live lightly in hospitality, pacing, room materials, table etiquette, and rights-clean original ambience. A conditional non-human robot is appropriate for the AI dealer.
2. **First playable:** One Strict Table, four seats, deterministic deal-to-score hand, legal-information AI opponents, AI Dealer/Human Host selection, explicit commit interactions, accessibility controls, and no network claim.
3. **Engine and tools:** Keep the TypeScript/React browser build as a reversible interaction and rules proof. Do not mistake it for the final Quest runtime. Evaluate a Quest-native OpenXR/Meta toolchain only after this technical candidate and human task evidence establish the interaction contract.
4. **Rules architecture:** Keep the headless pure reducer, seeded deck, explicit manifest, public-seat projections, action replay, and invariant tests independent of rendering and future networking.
5. **Offline/network sequence:** Finish and critique local play first. A future private-room server must own deck, turns, reconnection, AI substitution, and authoritative events before multiplayer acceptance.
6. **Dealer/host boundary:** The robot AI dealer advances legal flow without privileged card knowledge. A Human Host manages invitations, room tone, seating, mute, and between-hand pacing but never changes committed deals, cards, bids, tricks, or scores.
7. **Principal risks:** Rules variants presented as universal, browser-only concealed-state claims, insufficient Quest input evidence, unreadable dense table chrome, cultural signaling through stereotypes, and rights assumptions around music.
8. **Deferred work:** Meta Avatar SDK integration, production VR/MR runtime, hand/controller parity, authoritative networking, voice moderation, event orchestration, tutorial, shared audio, and multiple environments.
9. **Failure criteria:** Any scoring/replay inconsistency, opponent-card exposure, host authority over committed play, assigned demographic characters, unlicensed asset, or inability of experienced players to recognize and complete the launch flow triggers reshaping or kickback.
10. **Proof before production:** Exact candidate tests and browser playthrough, then experienced Bid Whist, beginner, accessibility, host, and cultural-advisor human sessions. Do not label the project complete before that acceptance.

## Strongest alternative

Start immediately in a Quest-native engine. That could reduce later porting work, but it would slow validation of rules and interaction, mix device/toolchain risk with game-design risk, and weaken the current two-hour proof objective. The reversible browser candidate is the better present experiment as long as its limitations remain explicit.
